01 / About

Problems that cross layers.

I'm a software engineer at the National Bank of Egypt, working on the digital banking platform used by retail and corporate customers on mobile and web.

I work across the whole channel — the banking services customers use, the logic and flows behind them, the services and APIs that feed them, the database side, and getting releases onto the servers. A lot of it starts from a Figma design and ends as a feature running in production.

Most issues in banking don't live in one place. I trace them from the screen through the services and integrations down to the data until I find the actual cause. That's the most useful thing I've built here.

What decides whether any of it worked is the person holding the phone — whether what they came to do takes less effort than it did before.

Before the bank I freelanced for a couple of years, building websites and single-page applications for clients. That's where my JavaScript and React foundation comes from.

Transfer Revamp

Moving money is the reason most customers open a banking app. Rebuilding that part of the channel wasn't on anyone's plan — I put the case together, built a proof of concept to show it could be done on the platform we already had, and stayed with it until it went live.

The hard part was never the design. It was rebuilding something people depend on every single day, while they're depending on it, and having no one feel the change.

  • Every transfer journey rebuilt: own accounts with currency conversion, other banks by account number and IBAN, wallets, instant payments, donations, cards, scheduled and multiple transfers
  • New components built beside the existing ones rather than on top, so live services stayed untouched through a phased rollout
  • Routing rules for each payment rail with the fees and validation each one requires, and live currency conversion behind a customer-facing amount
  • Reference data reconciled against the payment network, and the service configuration behind every screen
  • Carried through to production: deployment steps, database scripts, a rollback plan agreed before go-live, and the acceptance cycles that signed it off
  • Delivered across mobile, tablet and web, for retail and corporate customers, in Arabic and English
